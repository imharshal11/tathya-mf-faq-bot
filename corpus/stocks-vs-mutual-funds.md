# Stocks vs mutual funds (educational)

This comparison is generic investing literacy for class. It is not a recommendation to buy anything.

## Stocks (shares)

A stock is a small ownership slice of one listed company. If you buy a stock, your result depends mainly on that company and how the market prices it. Prices move during market hours. You can gain if the price rises or if the company pays a dividend; you can lose if the price falls. Concentration risk is high if most of your money sits in one or two names.

## Mutual funds

A mutual fund pools money from many people and invests it according to a stated mandate (for example large-cap equity, debt, or hybrid). A professional fund manager (or an index rule) chooses the underlying securities. You buy **units** of the fund at NAV (net asset value), which updates on a schedule, not tick-by-tick like a stock during the day.

Diversification is the usual reason people use funds: one purchase can spread money across many securities. You still take market risk. Equity funds can fall sharply. Debt funds have interest-rate and credit risk.

## Side-by-side (as used in this demo)

- **What you own:** one company (stock) vs a basket managed to a mandate (fund).
- **How you buy on an app like Groww:** search the stock or fund, place an order / investment, wait for settlement or allotment of units.
- **SIP:** more common for funds (fixed amount on a date). Stocks are more often bought as one-off orders, though some apps support systematic stock purchases.
- **Research load:** stock picking needs company-level work; fund picking needs mandate, costs (expense ratio), and past process — not just last year’s return.

Neither product is “safer” in all cases. A volatile stock can lose more than a diversified equity fund; a fund that holds risky credit can still lose money. This corpus does not tell you which to buy.
